Where a new barn would go

The proposal

  • 40 × 60 post-frame (2,400 sf), 16 ft sidewalls
  • 14 × 14 and 12 × 12 overhead doors on the south wall
  • Half for the Airstream, vehicles and a workshop; half a heated gym, cold plunge, sauna and recovery wing

Why there

  • Setbacks: ~85 ft from every lot line (20 ft required), behind the house as the township requires
  • Access: only ~110 ft of new drive off the existing loop
  • Topography: gentle 1–2% fall to the northwest; light grading, swale to the NW
  • Sun: south-facing doors and apron melt snow; the long south roof suits solar; winter wind hits the back wall
  • Trees and views: open ground, no clearing; keeps the north meadow open

Cost (published estimates)

  • ~$75K basic shell + slab
  • ~$140K insulated, heated and plumbed
  • ~$250K+ fully finished gym/recovery wing

Sources: Mich Barn, Great Lakes Post Frame. Get a topo survey before siting.

Alternative

The vacant north parcel gives more privacy and room for a 40 × 80, but needs a ~400 ft drive, sits lower and wetter, and can't hold an accessory building on its own unless the two parcels are combined.

Zoning

East Bay Charter Township ordinance, Agricultural district.

Outbuildings (§5.1)

  • Side or rear yard, ≥20 ft from side/rear lines, ≥5 ft from the house, ≤35 ft tall; land use permit required
  • Total accessory floor area: 4,000 sf on the 3.47-ac house parcel; 6,000 sf if the parcels are combined (over 5 ac)
  • Buildings bigger than the house need screening unless naturally screened

Guest quarters (§4.1)

  • One accessory dwelling allowed by right with a land use permit
  • Up to 1,000 sf (or 52% of the house, if less); can be in an accessory building
  • Owner must live on the property; no short-term rental of either unit
  • Must match the house; no exterior stairways

Land and road

  • AG setbacks: front 30, side 10, rear 35 ft; 20% max coverage
  • Horses allowed on lots over 5 acres
  • Paved county road; no special assessments or road projects found
  • Both legal descriptions reference easements
  • Taxes today ~$3.4K/yr; after a sale roughly $7.2K–$8.1K as a homestead (estimate)

Tuesday tour checklist

What to look at and measure on the showing. The floor plan and barn sketch on this site are estimates from listing plans and photos; these items check them on site.

Inspect the house

  • Roof: 2022 "premium" shingles; ask for brand and warranty; check the farmhouse/addition joint
  • Basement: 1900 fieldstone and block walls; water lines, efflorescence, sump, sill plate and joists
  • Farmhouse vs addition: sloping floors, cracks where they meet; windows are original wood sashes with new cords, not replacements; ask what electrical and plumbing work was done and when
  • Heat and cooling: boiler make and install date (the photo shows modern zoned copper piping and plastic venting), water heater age; no A/C listed (mini-splits later)
  • Well and septic: the listing says a new well about two years ago; confirm how many wells there are, where each is (house and barn?), and get the well record; septic location, age, last pumping
  • Porches and the steep farmhouse stair

Garage and Airstream

  • 2-car attached: measure size and door height
  • The 2022 Airstream Interstate 24X 4x4 on 2021 Mercedes Sprinter 3500 chassis (24′6″ × 9′9″) won't fit today; the new barn needs a 12 ft-tall door, a 28 ft+ bay, a 5–6 in slab and a 30 A or 50 A outlet

Power, heat and charging

  • Photograph the main panel label: brand, main breaker amps, age
  • Count open breaker slots (room for a 60 A Tesla Wall Connector circuit and a Powerwall)
  • Note the electric meter location and which utility's meter it is
  • Garage wall space for Powerwall 3 units and the Wall Connector
  • Generator inlet or transfer switch present?
  • Natural gas meter and service size (for a future barn heater or standby generator)

Two-car minimum

  • Measure each bay: length, width and door width/height for the 2023 Model Y and the Cadillac XT5
  • Confirm doors open fully with both parked

Barn site → gym and recovery

  • Walk the flat meadow on the house parcel: drainage, distance from lot lines (20 ft minimum) and from the road
  • Small original barn: size (~12 × 16 est.), foundation, electric, separate meter?
  • Look at the lower, wetter north parcel and the ~400 ft drive it would need
  • Gas service size for a future barn heater or generator

Two parcels and easements

  • Confirm both parcels convey and whether to combine them (more barn area allowed over 5 acres)
  • Both legal descriptions mention an easement: find out where and what for
  • Survey and title commitment

Things to confirm

  • Guest unit: up to 1,000 sf allowed by right with a land use permit; owner must live there; no short-term rental
  • Receipts or permits for the roof (2022), well, boiler, electrical, plumbing, the expansion wing and the steam shower; seller disclosure
  • Current tax bill on both parcels
  • Flood zone or wetland on the north parcel
  • Any farm or hunting lease; road right-of-way
  • What's planned on the cleared land next door
  • What conveys

Verify the site's estimates in person

  • Stair locations: farmhouse stair from the living room, basement stair at center
  • Bedrooms: primary on the main floor, two upstairs; the basement studio has no egress window, so it isn't a bedroom
  • Room sizes and furniture fit: primary is 11′9″ × 11′5″ (queen fits, king tight)
  • Garage size (estimated) and small barn size
  • Orientation: the drone view looks north toward the bay; barn meadow position

Red flags

  • Basement water or foundation movement
  • Old knob-and-tube wiring
  • Unpermitted addition or steam shower
  • Septic failure signs; traffic noise from Garfield

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Lowest list price of the three; most house per dollar
  • Under 10 minutes to Traverse City; TCAPS schools
  • Agricultural zoning with a guest unit allowed by right
  • Flat, open meadow: easy to build the barn you want
  • 2022 roof, newer well, paved road

Cons

  • No building for the Airstream or gym today
  • Dated interior; small primary bedroom
  • 1900 fieldstone basement, original windows, no A/C
  • Kids' rooms up a steep farmhouse stair
  • Busy county road, ball fields and active land clearing next door
